# Feature 023 — Supermarket and Shopping Pilot Brief

This is the content brief for the first Practical Dutch topic. It is not final
learner copy. The author must produce and review one atomic A1/A2 package under
the `shopping-and-cafes` Practical life pathway.

## Topic boundary

```text
Topic: Supermarket and shopping
Topic ID: topic.supermarket-shopping
Parent pathway: shopping-and-cafes
Lesson IDs: lesson.supermarket-shopping.a1 and lesson.supermarket-shopping.a2
Levels: A1 and A2
First path: approximately 5–7 minutes per level
```

The pair must be authored together and released together. A draft or failed
level keeps the complete topic out of production.

## A1 — Find products and pay

### Learner outcome

The learner can handle a basic supermarket visit: locate products, ask simple
availability or price questions, request a quantity, find checkout, pay by
card, and handle a basic receipt or bag question.

### Communicative intentions

Select the smallest useful set that supports the outcome:

1. Get an employee's attention politely.
2. Ask where a product is.
3. Understand a simple location response.
4. Ask whether an alternative is available.
5. Ask what something costs or whether it is on offer.
6. Request a basic quantity.
7. Find the checkout.
8. Ask to pay by card.
9. Ask for or decline a receipt or bag.
10. Close the interaction politely.

### Language focus

Primary pattern: `Waar kan ik ... vinden?`

Use the frame with several product substitutions. A supporting observation may
mention Dutch question word order, but the lesson must not become a general
inversion lesson.

### Vocabulary territory

Use only items required by the final outcomes. Candidate territory includes:

- `het gangpad`, `de kassa`, `de aanbieding`, `de bon`, `de tas`, `de prijs`;
- `de melk`, `de havermelk`, `vinden`, `kosten`, `betalen`, `pinnen`; and
- `nodig hebben`.

### Candidate chunks

- `Waar kan ik ... vinden?`
- `Hebben jullie ook ...?`
- `in de aanbieding`
- `Ik wil graag ...`
- `met pin betalen`
- `Mag ik ..., alstublieft?`
- `Ik heb geen ... nodig`

### Keep out

Do not make A1 depend on detailed complaints, refunds, ingredients/allergens,
long service-desk exchanges, complex measurements, or one repeated product
name in every sentence.

## A2 — Product information and problem resolution

### Learner outcome

The learner can ask for detailed product information, compare options, report a
straightforward price or product problem, and request a polite resolution.

### Communicative intentions

Select the smallest useful set that supports the outcome:

1. Ask whether a product contains an ingredient.
2. Ask whether it suits a dietary need or allergy.
3. Compare two products or explain a preference.
4. Ask when an unavailable item will return.
5. Clarify a shelf price or promotion condition.
6. Report an incorrectly charged item.
7. Ask an employee to check the receipt or price.
8. Explain a quality or expiry-date problem.
9. Ask whether an item can be exchanged or returned.
10. Request a polite resolution.
11. Respond to a basic service-desk question.

### Language focus

Primary pattern: `Kunt u controleren of ...?`

Use `Ik zie dat ...; kunt u dit controleren?` as supporting material only. The
lesson may identify polite `u` language as one supporting observation.

### Vocabulary territory

Use only items required by the final outcomes. Candidate territory includes:

- `de ingrediënten`, `de allergie`, `glutenvrij`, `lactosevrij`;
- `geschikt voor`, `de houdbaarheidsdatum`, `de kassabon`, `de voorraad`;
- `verkeerd aangeslagen`, `controleren`, `vergelijken`, `terugbrengen`,
  `ruilen`, `terugbetalen`, `beschadigd`, and `verlopen`.

### Candidate chunks

- `Kunt u controleren of ...?`
- `Is dit geschikt voor ...?`
- `Zit er ... in?`
- `niet meer op voorraad`
- `verkeerd aangeslagen`
- `op de kassabon`
- `Ik wil dit graag terugbrengen.`
- `Kan ik dit ruilen?`

### Keep out

Avoid legal claims about consumer rights, medical advice about allergies,
aggressive complaint language, long conditional arguments, and B1/B2
consumer-rights or bureaucratic language. Do not reuse the A1 checkout
interaction as the main A2 context.
