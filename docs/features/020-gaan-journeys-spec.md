# Feature 020: `gaan` Verb Journeys

## Problem Statement

DutchMate learners need to use `gaan` in ordinary Dutch, but a conjugation
table does not teach the decisions carried by this irregular, high-utility
verb. Learners must distinguish movement and departure, destinations, plans,
near-future phrasing, past movement, and completed movement with `zijn`.

English also encourages misleading one-to-one tense assumptions. A learner may
translate every English “go,” “went,” “will go,” or “have gone” into one Dutch
pattern, miss the irregular forms `ga`, `gaat`, `gaan`, and `ging`, or use
`hebben` where an authored movement example requires `zijn`.

The existing Verb Journey surface needs a fourth verb package that teaches
these practical decisions without resetting the existing `werken`, `zijn`, or
`hebben` packs, creating a general auxiliary course, or adding another learning
system.

## Solution

Add an authored, versioned `gaan` Verb Journey pack to the existing additive
multi-pack registry. The package contains six complete, playable journeys
organised around learner meanings:

1. Going somewhere and leaving.
2. Plans and near-future `gaan` plus an infinitive.
3. Past movement with `ging` and `gingen`.
4. Completed movement with `ben/is/zijn gegaan`.
5. Future and conditional movement with `zal gaan` and `zou gaan`.
6. Earlier and completed-future movement as clearly labelled A2 reference
   material.

The package keeps stories primarily first-person while adding bounded
person/inversion contrasts where they are necessary for real use. It keeps
Dutch as the learning language with English and Telugu support and remains
within the A0/Pre-A1 through A2 foundation progression.

Every journey follows the existing complete path: a five-line learner story,
notice and choice, the shared eight-form Dutch Verb Map or English comparison,
five deterministic click-only practice decisions, at most two targeted repairs,
evidence, completion, and a return path. Later/reference priority changes
recommendation status only; it never creates placeholder content or a dead end.

The eight-form map and twelve-pattern English comparison are part of the same
pack. The comparison explains meaning-preserving and Everyday Dutch rather
than asserting one-to-one English/Dutch tense equivalence. Form progress is
derived from canonical target-form records and remains separate from journey
completion.

## Implementation Decisions

- Add `gaan` as a fourth authored `VerbJourneyPack`, selected by stable verb
  identity through the existing additive registry. Preserve all existing pack
  IDs, evidence keys, export/import records, migration behavior, and review
  semantics.
- Model `gaan` as an irregular verb with the existing eight Dutch form codes:
  OTT, OVT, VTT, VVT, OTTT, OVTT, VTTT, and VVTT. The authored examples use
  reviewed forms such as `ga`, `ging`, `ben gegaan`, `was gegaan`, `zal gaan`,
  `zou gaan`, `zal gegaan zijn`, and `zou gegaan zijn` in context.
- Keep the eight-form Dutch Verb Map as reference data. It is not an eight-
  lesson requirement or a beginner mastery gate.
- Provide exactly six complete journeys with stable `gaan` identities. Every
  journey owns five story lines, one notice, five core exercises, bounded
  repairs, target skills, a map/comparison destination, evidence, completion,
  and a return path.
- Keep stories primarily first-person. Allow only the bounded person and
  inversion contrasts defined by the qualification matrix; do not expand to
  full-paradigm uncued production.
- Treat `gaan`’s perfect movement construction with `zijn` as authored,
  bounded content. Selected contrasts may mention a `hebben` construction, but
  the package does not teach general auxiliary selection or participle
  formation.
- Include all twelve English comparison patterns. Each record has separate
  meaning-preserving and Everyday Dutch roles, each with a complete NL/EN/TE
  localized sentence and Dutch form or construction identity. Matching roles
  remain distinct records at the role level.
- Give each English comparison one authored cue with bounded kind, display
  text, short meaning, and safe token data. Cue emphasis is limited to tokens
  authored for and literally present in the relevant Dutch variant.
- Use a new additive content version for the `gaan` package and any required
  comparison contract. Do not silently mutate stable IDs or regrade existing
  evidence. Incompatible changes must be explicit and atomic on migration
  failure.
- Derive learner-facing form progress from canonical target-form records. A
  journey with evidence contributes one slot per declared target form; the
  denominator is the pack’s eight canonical form records. Journey completion is
  rendered separately.
- Reuse the existing deterministic click-only practice evaluator. The five
  core families are meaning/situation, controlled construction, natural
  translation or conversational choice, map placement, and delayed/recombined
  word order. Repairs are authored and capped at two per incorrect path.
- Preserve duplicate-token identity, selected-token order, removal by index,
  retry, reset, exact accepted alternatives, and deterministic feedback.
- Record evidence through the existing learning-record seam. After queued
  writes, stale-write reconciliation, or completion submission, refresh the
  canonical persisted record before rendering overview, directory, Today, or
  Daily Five summaries.
- Extend the existing Lessons, verb directory, overview, journey, notice, map,
  English comparison, completion, Today, and Daily Five routes. Do not add a
  top-level popup tab or a second scheduler.
- Preserve the multilingual map presentation contract: cards show only tense
  code, one status symbol, and visible NL/EN/TE prefixes; details show full
  names, status labels, ordered content, and one localized common-use section.
- Preserve the English comparison presentation contract: grouped records,
  separate role labels, safe cue emphasis, detail navigation, and a return path
  to the active journey.
- Preserve accessible selected state, visible status legend names, keyboard
  operation, nearest-only detail scrolling, readable secondary languages, and
  narrow-popup containment.
- Do not create a new prototype. The existing Verb Journey interaction is the
  approved behavior reference; a prototype may be reconsidered only if
  implementation discovery finds a materially different interaction need.
- Complete the authoring matrix and qualification record before implementation.
  Record Dutch reviewer, date, sources, English/Telugu clarity checks,
  allowed-person scope, progress contribution, content versions, and deferred
  ideas.

## Testing Decisions

- Tests must exercise external behavior at the highest stable existing seam.
  They should prove what a learner can reach, select, complete, and resume;
  they should not lock tests to private helper layout or incidental DOM
  structure beyond the approved accessibility and presentation contract.
- Extend the multi-pack content validation and qualification tests to prove
  exactly eight unique Dutch forms, twelve English comparison records, six
  ordered journeys, five story lines per journey, valid target spans, stable
  references, complete NL/EN/TE support, reviewed cues, notices, and no
  forbidden scope leakage.
- Test the `gaan` pack alongside `werken`, `zijn`, and `hebben` so that the
  shared validator proves all registered packs still satisfy the same
  multilingual and map/comparison contracts.
- Extend practice tests to prove every `gaan` journey returns its own five
  questions, all five exercise families are represented, accepted alternatives
  are deterministic, repairs are owned and capped, retry/reset works, and
  duplicate tokens remain distinct occurrences.
- Extend progress tests to prove target-form slot counting for repeated target
  forms, multi-form journeys, empty evidence, different future pack shapes,
  and separate journey completion. The denominator must come from the pack’s
  canonical form records.
- Extend learning-record and background tests to prove additive evidence,
  idempotency, stale submission protection, immediate post-completion refresh,
  migration atomicity, unknown old IDs, export/import compatibility, Daily Five
  eligibility, and preservation of all existing verb records.
- Extend popup/Lessons integration tests to prove directory discovery,
  `gaan` selection, overview progress, all six journey routes, notice-to-map
  focus, map/comparison return, completion return, Today activity, Saved
  non-expansion, back navigation, and absence of audio UI.
- For the multilingual map, assert all eight cards have NL/EN/TE, cards do not
  expose full Dutch names or visible status words, the legend has visible names,
  selected state is accessible, detail sections appear in the approved order,
  common use is one localized section, and selection scrolls the nearest detail
  panel only.
- For the English comparison lens, assert all twelve records are reachable,
  each has both Dutch roles, role-specific form identities are shown, cues are
  authored and safe, English/Telugu support is present, and mismatch
  explanations remain visible.
- Add focused regression coverage for the longest VTTT/VVTT sentences, Telugu
  wrapping, narrow-popup containment, keyboard navigation, and accessible
  feedback. Manual review must cover Chromium and Firefox at normal and 125%
  zoom where the repository workflow supports those checks.
- Run the repository verification ladder: focused content/practice tests,
  popup tests, typecheck, the full test suite, build/package checks, and
  whitespace validation. Manual QA must include an incorrect answer, retry,
  non-default journey, later/reference journey, duplicate-token question,
  map/comparison return, completion, and journey-list return.
- Language qualification is separate from structural testing. A fluent Dutch
  reviewer must review every learner-visible Dutch string, and English/Telugu
  support must receive independent meaning and clarity review before release.

## Out of Scope

- A broad cross-verb auxiliary course or general participle-formation course.
- A full course covering the idiomatic range of *gaan*, including pragmatic
  expressions outside the approved movement and planning boundary.
- Audio, speech recognition, pronunciation feedback, or listening practice.
- Runtime LLM grading, generation, translation, tense inference, or network
  dependency.
- Typed or free-form answers; the package remains click-only.
- A new top-level popup destination, standalone Verb Gym, standalone Sentence
  Forge, second scheduler, parallel mastery model, or parallel evidence store.
- Speculative Saved-to-`gaan` resolution when existing context cannot classify a
  saved form safely.
- A new clickable prototype unless the approved interaction contract proves
  insufficient during implementation discovery.
- CEFR claims above A2, independent-production claims, formal proficiency
  certification, or a promise that journey completion equals mastery.
- Destructive migration, reset of existing learner history, re-keying of
  existing packs, or incompatible silent content replacement.
- Expansion of the approved person scope into uncued full-paradigm production.

## Further Notes

- The canonical feature plan records the approved product boundary, branch,
  glossary decision, parking-lot entries, qualification gate, and approval
  sequence. This specification carries that understanding into implementation-
  ready behavior and test contracts.
- The feature remains docs-first. Ticket breakdown and GitHub child issues are
  a separate approval gate after this specification and its seams are reviewed.
- Existing DutchMate design tokens, interaction patterns, and persistence
  contracts remain the source of truth. The `gaan` content package must adapt
  to them rather than introducing verb-specific UI.
- The release gate is package-wide: `gaan` content, shared map/comparison
  rendering, all registered packs, learner-history compatibility, structural
  validation, linguistic review, automated tests, and manual popup QA must all
  pass before delivery.
