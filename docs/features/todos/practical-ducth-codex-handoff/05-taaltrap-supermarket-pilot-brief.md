# Taaltrap Pilot Authoring Brief

## Topic family

```text
Topic: Supermarket and shopping
Topic ID: topic.supermarket-shopping
Slug: supermarket-shopping
Domain: everyday-errands
Levels: A1 and A2
```

Author both levels together so their scope is intentionally separated.

---

# A1 — Find products and pay

## Learner outcome

The learner can handle a basic supermarket visit: locate products, ask simple availability/price questions, request a quantity, and complete a straightforward checkout exchange.

## Required communicative intentions

1. Politely get an employee’s attention.
2. Ask where a product is.
3. Understand a simple aisle/location response.
4. Ask whether an alternative product is available.
5. Ask what something costs.
6. Ask whether something is on offer.
7. Request a basic quantity.
8. Find the checkout.
9. Ask to pay by card.
10. Ask for or decline a receipt/bag.
11. Close the interaction politely.

The sentence bank should select 8–12 of these without becoming repetitive.

## Suggested contextual input

A short exchange with an employee plus a short checkout interaction.

## Candidate vocabulary

Use only items needed by the final content:

- `het gangpad`
- `de kassa`
- `de aanbieding`
- `de bon`
- `de tas`
- `de prijs`
- `de melk`
- `de havermelk`
- `vinden`
- `kosten`
- `betalen`
- `pinnen`
- `nodig hebben`

## Candidate chunks

- `Waar kan ik … vinden?`
- `Hebben jullie ook …?`
- `in de aanbieding`
- `Ik wil graag …`
- `met pin betalen`
- `Mag ik …, alstublieft?`
- `Ik heb geen … nodig`

## Primary pattern candidate

```text
Waar kan ik + object + infinitive?
```

The author may choose a better single primary pattern after reviewing the complete lesson, but must document the reason.

## Exercise priorities

At minimum:

- Two vocabulary-fill exercises
- One collocation-fill exercise
- One pattern-fill exercise
- One token-order question
- One product-substitution transfer
- One checkout dialogue completion
- One best polite response
- Meaning/situation questions to meet total coverage

## Avoid

- Detailed complaints or refunds
- Ingredient/allergen questions
- Long customer-service interactions
- Complex measurements unless essential
- Too many product names
- Making every sentence use `melk`

# A2 — Product information and problem resolution

## Learner outcome

The learner can ask for detailed product information, compare options, report a straightforward pricing/product problem, and request a polite resolution.

## Required communicative intentions

1. Ask whether a product contains an ingredient.
2. Ask whether a product is suitable for a dietary need or allergy.
3. Compare two products or explain a preference.
4. Ask when an unavailable item will return.
5. Clarify a shelf price or promotion condition.
6. Report that an item was charged incorrectly.
7. Ask an employee to check the receipt or price.
8. Explain a quality or expiry-date problem.
9. Ask whether an item can be exchanged or returned.
10. Request a refund or suitable resolution politely.
11. Respond to a basic service-desk question.

The sentence bank should select 8–12 of these and remain A2 rather than drifting into B1 legal/consumer-rights language.

## Suggested contextual input

A product-information exchange followed by a short customer-service-desk conversation about an incorrect price or unsuitable product.

## Candidate vocabulary

Use only items needed by the final content:

- `de ingrediënten`
- `de allergie`
- `glutenvrij`
- `lactosevrij`
- `geschikt voor`
- `de houdbaarheidsdatum`
- `de kassabon`
- `de voorraad`
- `verkeerd aangeslagen`
- `controleren`
- `vergelijken`
- `terugbrengen`
- `ruilen`
- `terugbetalen`
- `beschadigd`
- `verlopen`

## Candidate chunks

- `Kunt u controleren of …?`
- `Is dit geschikt voor …?`
- `Zit er … in?`
- `niet meer op voorraad`
- `verkeerd aangeslagen`
- `op de kassabon`
- `Ik wil dit graag terugbrengen.`
- `Kan ik dit ruilen?`

## Primary pattern candidates

Choose exactly one:

```text
Kunt u controleren of ...?
```

or:

```text
Ik zie dat ...; kunt u dit controleren?
```

## Supporting observation candidate

Polite `u` language at the service desk.

## Exercise priorities

At minimum:

- Ingredient/property vocabulary fills
- Collocation fills: `geschikt voor`, `op voorraad`, `op de kassabon`
- A polite-pattern completion
- A sentence-repair exercise
- A service-desk dialogue completion
- A best-response exercise
- A transfer task with a different product/problem
- A comprehension item distinguishing price, ingredient, and return problems

## Avoid

- Legal claims about consumer rights
- Aggressive complaint language
- Advanced medical advice about allergies
- Long conditional arguments
- Reusing the A1 checkout interaction as the main A2 content
- Making A2 merely longer versions of A1 sentences

---

# Pair-level review

Before approval confirm:

- A1 focuses on finding, asking, buying, and paying.
- A2 focuses on product detail, comparison, and problem resolution.
- The levels do not share the same primary pattern.
- Repeated essential vocabulary is purposeful.
- A2 contains genuinely new problem-solving language.
- Both contain at least 12 exercises.
- Both contain strong vocabulary fill-in-the-blank coverage.
- Both fit the popup and can be completed in short sessions.
